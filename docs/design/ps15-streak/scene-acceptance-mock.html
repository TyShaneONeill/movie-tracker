Streak Camera — the screening room

PS-15 · streak camera · round 5 · the screening room

The glass is the screen.
You're sitting in the room.

Round 3's projection could only ever show the camera in profile. It had an elevation — look up at it, look down at it — but no azimuth, so nothing could turn it, and the lens stayed a white block stuck on the side instead of an aperture pointed anywhere. This build replaces it with a real two-angle axonometric, which is what finally lets the light come toward you.

The couch and the projector are pushed back against the wall and the lamp is turned on you. The number isn't painted on anything in the room — it's the throw landing on the phone's own glass, in front of the couch, which is why it flickers with the lamp and why the couch reads as a silhouette. Whoever earned the streak is sitting in that seat.

what azimuth buys, and what it costs 0° is head-on, 90° is pure profile. Anywhere between and the artwork foreshortens by sin φ — the reels turn to ellipses, the body narrows, and you read a solid that has been rotated rather than one that has been redrawn. Below about 35° it stops being recognisable, because this camera's identity is entirely in its silhouette. That's a property of the artwork, not a bug in the maths, and it's why the slider stops where it does.

Where it points

the fix

The camera's own facing. Under 90° swings the lens toward you and the beam comes forward out of the frame; over 90° turns it away, which is what a projector aimed at a wall actually does.

A pure mirror. It lands entirely in the matrix — it cancels out of the depth sweep — so nothing downstream has to know about it.

Yours, not the camera's. How far above the floor you're standing.

The projector being aimed, nose up. Without it the beam leaves at whatever angle azimuth happens to give and the number has to be shoved to meet it — which is a lie, just a subtler one than round 2's.

Still read-only, still derived. atan2(cos φ · sin ε, sin φ) — the lens axis falls out of the same two angles that draw the body, so they cannot disagree.

The room

tripod, couch, rug

At zero the couch leaves. It's the only thing in the room that stands for a person, so losing it costs the whole conceit.

Everything the projector isn't doing. Push it up and the room stops being a cinema.

Behind the number

six treatments

Not a position — a distance. The number sits in the beam, so when the projector is aimed at the wall it lands on the wall, and when it's aimed at you it floats in front of the couch. One line, no special case.

The lamp

and the day turning over

Hard cuts on an irregular beat, driving the beam, the lens glow and the number as one light. Evenly spaced flicker reads as a broken loop; a soft fade reads as a dimmer, not a shutter.

What the user sees at midnight. The roll is on the glyph only — the beam and the room hold still, because the light didn't change, the count did.

Shipping notes
read before costing this

        

          

            
01 · the room is a mock, the camera is an asset

            
The couch, rug and tripod exist to prove the concept reads, not to ship as SVG. If the room survives review, it should become one exported raster backdrop with the number and the beam composited live on top. Shipping the room as vector buys nothing and costs a few hundred nodes on every open.

          

          

            
02 · azimuth has a floor

            
The slider stops at 34° deliberately. Below that the profile foreshortens past recognition — you get a red lozenge. If a true head-on projector is ever wanted, it needs a second piece of artwork (a front elevation), not a lower number here. Worth knowing before anyone promises a spin animation.

          

          

            
03 · one matrix still does the work

            
Both angles collapse into matrix(-sinφ, -cosφ·sinε, 0, cosε, …), and the depth sweep into a local offset of (-cotφ, -tanε/sinφ) per unit. Still no per-vertex projection, still authored in the original 200×160 space, still inherits any revision to camera-active.svg.

          

          

            
04 · the number is native text

            
Solid and Extrude are offset <Text> copies — no filters, identical on both platforms. Halo and Silhouette need a real blur and land back in the Android filter problem that bit the numeral halo. Pick from the first two if it has to ship as designed.
